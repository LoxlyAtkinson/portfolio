How I work

The work below is ordinary application software. What is not ordinary is the amount of effort that goes into proving it actually works, because most of my production incidents have come from believing something shipped when it had not.

A green build is not a deploy

One client deploy reported success and left a ten day old homepage serving. So the pipeline for that project now has twenty five gates, and every gate names the specific recorded failure it exists to prevent. Gate twenty one fetches the live homepage, reads the bundle hash out of it, refetches that chunk and sha256 compares it against what was just built. The pipeline correctly refused to deploy on two of its first real runs.

A passing test is not a working screen

Across one platform there are 224 proof harnesses among 320 scripts, under a house rule that a user facing change is finished only when it has been observed working in a real browser. On my own site the proof checklist gets written before the screenshot is taken, not after. The last one scored itself five out of nine and listed four explicit failures rather than rounding up.

Postgres, written by hand

224 hand written migrations across one project, 35,653 lines of SQL and no ORM. 237 row level security policies, 265 indexes, 211 functions, 166 of them PL/pgSQL. An independent reviewer put the SQL at strong advanced applied Postgres, around seven out of ten, and noted only one recursive CTE in 35,000 lines and no EXPLAIN anywhere. That is a fair reading and I would rather print it than a self awarded grade.

Write down what went wrong

Four of these projects carry written engineering post mortems, indexed and categorised: 755, 457, 306 and 205 of them. They exist because the same mistake made twice is the expensive one. One blind design re audit caught three of its own earlier fixes being wrong, including a background colour that silently voided a whole palette conversion while still compiling cleanly.
