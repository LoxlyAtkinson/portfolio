## Grammar: Gallery / catalog

The page is a walkable collection of objects with museum labels, not an argument.

**Why the other seven lost:**

- **Filmic one-shot** forbids visible sequence and more than one entry point. This
  is a ten-page site whose nine case studies must each be reachable and linkable.
  A grammar that bans an index cannot host it. It is also the drift this skill
  exists to resist, and nothing in the interview asked for it.
- **Continuous world** requires worldflight mode, which the owner ruled out by
  choosing distinct scenes, and the brief is not a journey through a place.
- **Chaptered editorial** is the closest loser. It would suit the evidence, but it
  bans the full-bleed scrub hero and hard-cuts its grounds, and the owner asked
  for a clip he can scrub.
- **Live surface** bans `scrub` outright. The peak is a live-surface moment, which
  is why it runs in the chrome rather than defining the whole page.
- **Typographic poster** bans photographic ground. We are generating a world.
- **Split stage** needs two sides in tension the whole way. There is no second side.
- **Rhythmic cutlist** bans `pin` and `dwell` and wants twelve to twenty short
  sections at speed. The owner's energy answer is the opposite shape.

Gallery is not a fallback. It is named in the skill as fitting "a portfolio,
case studies, anything where the visitor's real question is what are the
options", and the existing evidence site already labels its work exactly the way
this grammar demands: client, sector, one line, status, live link, every object
on the same schema, with persuasion banned from the label.

**What the grammar forbids here:** the argument-shaped pinned type act, a single
hero claim, scrim copy over media, persuasion in any object label, kinetic
headlines, spotlight, magnet, and more than one `scrub` act.

## The signature move: the ledger

A persistent rail, fixed to the left edge on desktop and the bottom edge on
phones, present for the entire page. It is the navigation, which is what this
grammar requires of its chrome, and it is also a running record.

As the visitor passes each system, the ledger **stamps a line**: the domain, and
the HTTP status actually observed on 17 September 2026. Those lines stay. By the
close the rail is a complete receipt of everything just walked past, and every
line on it is clickable.

At the peak the same rail stops being an index and **becomes the deploy gate**:
twenty-five gates run down it under the reader's hand, each one naming the
failure it prevents, and gate 21 fetches the live page, compares the hash and
**REFUSES**. The rail holds on the refusal. Then it returns to being the index,
with the refusal stamped into it like every other receipt.

It is one bespoke thing, coded in the page against `--sc-p` and page scroll, with
the engine untouched. It is the navigation, the signature move, the peak and the
tell-someone sentence, which is what feel.md asks for when it says the signature
move and the peak must not point at different moments.

## World

Low-key cinematic, owner's choice. The subject is the decision that keeps it out
of generic AI territory: **real working South African interiors at night**, the
rooms these systems actually serve. A community radio desk. A union office after
hours. A GP waiting room before opening. A computer shop counter after closing.
Never servers, never abstract data, never glowing circuitry, never a person.

Style preamble, pasted verbatim into every prompt:

> Cinematic photography shot on 35mm anamorphic lenses. Shallow depth of field,
> high dynamic range, true blacks, matte film grain. Low-key lighting: one warm
> practical source visible in frame, cool ambient fill, deep falloff into
> shadow. Colour grade of deep charcoal and warm amber highlights with
> desaturated mid-tones. A real working South African interior, honest imperfect
> surfaces, worn and used, nobody in frame. Photographic realism. NOT 3D render,
> NOT clay, NOT illustration, NOT CGI, no digital glow, no plastic sheen, no
> lens flare, no legible text or writing anywhere in the image.
